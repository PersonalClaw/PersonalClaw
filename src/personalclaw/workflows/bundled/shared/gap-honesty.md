Where you could not establish something, say so inline rather than filling the gap with
something plausible.

This matters more than it sounds. A named gap is a small, cheap piece of work for whoever reads
this — they know exactly what to check. A gap filled with a confident-sounding guess is
indistinguishable from a fact, so it propagates: the next stage binds to it, a gate passes on it,
and the error surfaces somewhere that has no way to trace it back here.

Concretely:

- "I could not find where X is configured" beats naming a plausible file.
- "The two sources disagree and I could not tell which is current" beats averaging them into a
  hedge.
- "This assumes Y, which I did not verify" beats silently assuming Y.

An unanswered question, stated, is a result. It is not a failure to report one.
